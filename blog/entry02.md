#### Researching For Past Software Advancements 
I first decided to research more into past software advancements made in Animal Conservation and I found a lot of surprising results. One thing I realized was the importance of being more specific in my searches. When I searched up "animal technology apps", and "animal data apps" I had found no results. However, when I searched **"animal conservation technology apps"** I had a greater amount of searches and websites that gave me more in depth information. In most of the websites I researched I noticed a lot of animal conservation apps that would often use **user support** to help in gathering data on animal wildlife populations and their movements. However, one that stood out to me the most was the website called [eBird](https://ebird.org/home) which is an app that allows users to take pictures of wildlife and share that data to other users of the app. This sharing of images can allow scientists to receive more data and analytics on the locations of bird species, their populations, and their migration patterns. This information can provide new opportunities for future developments in animal conservation and can also allow scientists to take action to ensure that animal wildlife is protected. This app is recommended for anyone who is interested in birds and in the idea of animal conservation because it's free on ios android and PC. I then found another website called [wild track](https://www.wildtrack.org/) which is an app that specializes in the use of data analytics, artificial intelligence, and ecological knowledge to help in preserving wildlife. One thing that was really interesting to me about this app however was how accessible it was to the public and its reliable software technologies. One part of the app that really showed this was another app that was included named **FIT/Footprint Identification Technology** which uses photography on footprints of animals to help in tracking the data of animal population with just a simple picture of a footprint. With these non-invasive methods, data collecting can be a lot easier, less invasive to animal territories, and can further develop software advancements in animal conservation. However, this app is not accessible to everyone because you need experience that can allow you to sign in to the app. One last website I found for software advancements was [Earth Ranger](https://www.earthranger.com/) which has more of a focus on gathering data and analytics on animal habitats and their environment. One thing that really surprised me about a part of the website called the Ecoscope. The ecoscope is  free open source model that uses **GPS tracking** to track animal locations and their time spent on specific areas. Because of these tracking rangers can locate species of animals that are in the presence of poachers and can calculate populations of animal species. Earth ranger is accessible on all devices however it is heavily targeted towards security and less towards users who show interest in animal conservation. Rangers can use this app more to track animal locations and detect poachers. 

#### Researching For Past Hardware Advancements 
I then went on to research more into past hardware advancements made in animal conservation technology and I found a lot of surprising results. I first searched up **"Animal conservation hardware advancements"** and I found tons of different websites and organizations specializing in the protection of animals. However that wasn't what I was looking for so I looked in more deeper. I ended up finding an organization called [fauna and flora](https://www.fauna-flora.org/) which has a lot of information on specific hardware advancements made on animal conservation. I first found a hardware advancement which was the use of **Environmental DNA and Genomics**. It's one of those hardware advancements that combines bioengineering with hardware. Basically how it works is that natural samples from wildlife are collected and sent to labs who are able to transfer these natural samples into data that can reveal the presence of new and unknown animal species. However, this process can often be very lengthy and inefficient due to the amount of steps that it takes to acquire data and its cost but it can really depend on the working conditions, samples used, etc... Another advancement that stood out to me was the use of the **tagging and collaring/biologing of animals**. For this process to function an electronic tag is attached to the animal's body and their movement can be tracked with the tag on them. This in return allows for conservationists to help in the research, management, and security of animal populations in the wild. Collaring can often be cost effective due to its reusable materials that can be 3d printable. However it can also disturb wildlife due to animals needing to be held down in order to place the collar on their body. I then found another invention called the **camera trap** which although I talked about it before in my first blog entry I wanted to research more on it. The camera trap can be used as a way to track animal movements throughout the wild without disturbing any form of wildlife. Because of this, scientists can research more on different animal species that exist within the wild and also allow conservationists to secure and protect animal populations in the wild. Camera traps can be cheap and can average a price at around 30 to 100 dollars. However to get advanced cameras with full color they can be very costly at around 500 to 2000 dollars. The last hardware advancement that I found was the use of **drones** to scan out environments. Although the reason why drones are useful to animal conservation due to its high flying distance there's a lot more to it than it looks. Drones in animal conservation can be used to scan out models of landscapes which can be used to track which animals and species inhabit specific areas which can allow for more data on animal populations and land stability. Drones however can be very costly and can be difficult to control at times depending on the type of drone so it's important to choose drones that are reusable and cost effective within animal conservation.
